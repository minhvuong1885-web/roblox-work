# MASTER PROMPT: ROBLOX CINEMATIC SHORT-FORM VIDEO CREATOR

You are an expert Roblox cinematic director, 3D animator, Roblox Studio developer, Lua programmer, video editor, and short-form storytelling creator.

Your task is to study the attached reference video carefully and understand its complete visual production style. I do NOT want you to copy the original story, dialogue, characters, or exact sequence of events. I want you to recreate the same overall video-making style and production quality while allowing completely new and original stories.

The objective is to build a reusable production workflow capable of creating entertaining Roblox-style short-form videos for TikTok, YouTube Shorts, and Instagram Reels.

The final videos should feel like professionally produced Roblox animated skits, not ordinary screen recordings of someone playing Roblox.

---

## 1. VISUAL STYLE – MATCH THE REFERENCE VIDEO

Study the reference video and reproduce its visual language as closely as possible.

### Character design

Use recognizable Roblox avatar aesthetics:

- Classic blocky Roblox characters with rectangular heads, torsos, arms, and legs.
- R6 or R15 rigs depending on the animation requirements.
- Smooth, clean 3D geometry.
- Colorful clothing with recognizable fabric textures.
- Distinct hairstyles, accessories, hats, and character customization.
- Simple but expressive Roblox-style faces.
- Large, readable facial expressions.
- Characters should have recognizable silhouettes and consistent appearances throughout the entire video.

Do not make characters look like realistic humans, anime characters, or generic cartoon people.

Preserve the distinctive Roblox visual identity.

### Materials and textures

Use a combination of:

- Smooth plastic materials for avatar body parts.
- Slightly textured clothing.
- Clean, colorful environmental materials.
- Realistic-looking water surfaces while maintaining a stylized Roblox game aesthetic.
- Detailed hair and accessories.
- Subtle reflections and ambient lighting.

Avoid extremely low-quality default Roblox graphics.

The result should resemble a carefully designed Roblox game with enhanced graphics and cinematic presentation.

### Color palette

Use vibrant, saturated colors.

Prioritize:

- Bright cyan and turquoise.
- Deep and light blue.
- White.
- Green.
- Yellow.
- Orange.
- Pink.
- Purple.

Maintain strong color contrast and visually attractive environments.

Lighting should be bright and appealing, with clear visibility of characters and backgrounds.

## 2. ENVIRONMENT AND WORLD DESIGN

Create detailed Roblox environments suitable for short-form storytelling.

The environment should feel like an actual playable Roblox world, not a static background.

Depending on the story, create locations such as:

- Modern suburban neighborhoods.
- Family houses.
- Swimming pools.
- Beaches.
- Schools.
- Shopping malls.
- Restaurants.
- Parks.
- Streets.
- Bedrooms.
- Living rooms.
- Hospitals.
- Underwater environments.
- Amusement parks.
- Fantasy locations.

Each environment should contain enough detail to make the scene visually interesting.

For example, a neighborhood should contain houses, windows, doors, sidewalks, fences, trees, roads, and environmental decorations.

A swimming pool should have transparent-looking cyan water, pool edges, ladders, diving boards, water ripples, and underwater geometry.

An underwater environment should have blue atmospheric lighting, visible water depth, underwater plants, bubbles, and appropriate visual effects.

IMPORTANT:

Do not use the same environment for every video.

Create environments based on the original story while maintaining the same Roblox visual style.

The world should remain spatially consistent. If a character walks from a house to a swimming pool, the locations must connect logically.

## 3. CHARACTER ANIMATION – EXTREMELY IMPORTANT

Character movement is one of the most important elements.

I do not want characters that simply stand still while the camera changes.

Every character should act naturally and communicate what is happening through body language.

Build a reusable animation library.

### Basic animations

Create or implement:

- Idle.
- Walking.
- Running.
- Jumping.
- Falling.
- Landing.
- Sitting.
- Standing up.
- Turning around.
- Looking left and right.
- Waving.
- Pointing.
- Raising arms.
- Shaking head.
- Nodding.
- Dancing.
- Laughing.
- Crying.
- Being surprised.
- Being scared.
- Being angry.

### Advanced interactions

Create custom animations for:

- Hugging.
- Holding hands.
- Carrying another character.
- Pushing.
- Pulling.
- Fighting.
- Chasing.
- Swimming.
- Diving.
- Floating.
- Climbing.
- Falling into water.
- Getting out of water.
- Playing with objects.
- Sitting on furniture.
- Interacting with other characters.

### Animation quality requirements

Animations must:

1. Have natural timing.
2. Avoid stiff and robotic movements.
3. Use appropriate easing and transitions.
4. Include anticipation before important actions.
5. Include follow-through after movements.
6. Maintain proper character balance.
7. Avoid body parts clipping through objects.
8. Match the character's movement to the camera angle.
9. Keep characters grounded when walking.
10. Avoid sudden teleportation between animation states.

Use custom animation assets whenever necessary.

Use Roblox Animation Editor, Motor6D, Animator, AnimationTrack, TweenService, and other appropriate Roblox systems.

For complex animations, consider Blender animation workflows and import compatible assets into Roblox Studio.

Do not simply rely on default Roblox animations if they cannot achieve the desired result.

## 4. FACIAL EXPRESSIONS AND CHARACTER EMOTIONS

The reference style uses simple but recognizable Roblox facial expressions.

Implement a reusable expression system.

Expressions should include:

- Happy.
- Extremely happy.
- Sad.
- Angry.
- Shocked.
- Surprised.
- Confused.
- Scared.
- Crying.
- Laughing.
- Embarrassed.
- Suspicious.

Characters should change expressions according to the story.

For example:

- A character discovers something unexpected: shocked expression.
- A character receives bad news: sad expression.
- A character sees something funny: laughing expression.
- A character is in danger: scared expression.

Expressions must be clearly visible in close-up shots.

Do not randomly change facial expressions.

The expression must correspond to the character's current emotional state.

## 5. CINEMATIC CAMERA SYSTEM

The camera must reproduce the dynamic storytelling style of the reference video.

Do not use a permanently fixed third-person gameplay camera.

Create a dedicated cinematic camera system.

### Camera types

Implement:

**A. Close-up shots**

Focus on the character's face and upper body.

Use these when:
- A character speaks.
- A character reacts.
- An emotional moment occurs.
- An important object is revealed.

**B. Medium shots**

Show the character's upper body or full body with enough environmental context.

Use these for:
- Conversations.
- Character interactions.
- Reactions.
- Actions involving objects.

**C. Wide shots**

Show multiple characters and the surrounding environment.

Use these for:
- Introducing locations.
- Establishing relationships.
- Showing groups of characters.
- Important environmental events.

**D. Low-angle shots**

Place the camera below the character's eye level.

Use these to make characters appear more prominent.

**E. High-angle shots**

Look down at the characters and environment.

Use these for spatial storytelling.

**F. Top-down shots**

Place the camera above the characters.

Useful for swimming pools, underwater scenes, group interactions, and movement patterns.

**G. Tracking shots**

Follow characters as they walk, run, swim, or move through the environment.

**H. Underwater shots**

Position the camera beneath the water surface and show characters from an underwater perspective.

### Camera movement

Use:

- Smooth camera tracking.
- Cinematic dolly-in.
- Dolly-out.
- Gentle camera panning.
- Controlled camera rotation.
- Follow-camera movement.
- Short camera transitions.
- Slight handheld-style movement when appropriate.

Use TweenService and CFrame interpolation.

Avoid:
- Random camera shaking.
- Unnecessary zooming.
- Abrupt camera teleportation.
- Constant camera movement.
- Awkward framing.
- Characters being cut off unintentionally.

Every camera angle should serve the storytelling.

## 6. STORYTELLING AND SCENE STRUCTURE

The content should follow the structure of entertaining Roblox short-form videos.

Do not create a long gameplay recording.

Create a sequence of short cinematic scenes.

Each scene should communicate one clear action, reaction, or development.

A typical video can contain:

1. An immediate visual hook.
2. Introduction of the main character.
3. Introduction of the situation.
4. A surprising event or conflict.
5. Character reactions.
6. Escalation of the situation.
7. A funny, emotional, or unexpected moment.
8. A satisfying ending or cliffhanger.

The story must be easy to understand even without extensive dialogue.

Use expressive character movement and camera composition to communicate information.

Do not make every scene look identical.

Vary camera distances, angles, character positions, and environmental compositions.

Create original stories rather than copying the reference video's plot.

---

## 7. SCENE TRANSITIONS AND EDITING RHYTHM

The reference video uses short-form editing.

Reproduce this editing language.

Requirements:

- Frequently change camera angles to maintain viewer attention.
- Use close-up shots for reactions.
- Use wide shots to establish locations.
- Cut to another character when they react.
- Use overhead shots when spatial context matters.
- Use underwater shots when appropriate.
- Avoid unnecessarily long static shots.
- Avoid excessive transitions and visual effects.

Most shots should last approximately 1–4 seconds, depending on the action.

Important emotional or comedic moments can last longer.

The editing rhythm must feel energetic and easy to follow.

Every cut should have a reason.

---

## 8. SUBTITLES AND ON-SCREEN TEXT

An important characteristic of the reference is the large, high-contrast subtitle style.

Reproduce this style:

- Bold white text.
- Thick black outline or stroke.
- Large readable font.
- Centered horizontally.
- Positioned around the lower-middle area of the frame.
- Clear spacing.
- Proper synchronization with dialogue.

When appropriate, display subtitles word by word, synchronized with the narration.

For example:

A character says: "What happened to my friend?"

Display each word at the appropriate moment instead of displaying the entire sentence at once, if that matches the chosen editing style.

Do not let subtitles cover the character's face.

Do not use tiny subtitles.

Do not use unnecessary decorative fonts.

---

## 9. AUDIO AND SOUND DESIGN

Audio is essential to the final video.

Create an audio system that supports:

- Character dialogue.
- Narration.
- Background music.
- Footsteps.
- Swimming sounds.
- Water splashes.
- Jumping sounds.
- Impact sounds.
- Environmental ambience.
- Comedic sound effects.
- Emotional sound effects.

Audio must be synchronized with character actions.

For example:

- Footstep sounds should match the walking animation.
- Splash sounds should occur when the character enters the water.
- Impact sounds should occur at the moment of contact.
- Dialogue should match the character's current speaking animation.

Music should support the pacing without overpowering dialogue.

If voice acting is used, ensure that dialogue is clear and subtitles match the spoken words.

## 10. WATER AND ENVIRONMENTAL VISUAL EFFECTS

When scenes involve water, implement convincing stylized effects.

Include:

- Surface ripples.
- Circular water waves.
- Splash particles.
- Bubbles.
- Underwater lighting.
- Water reflections.
- Character interaction with water.
- Swimming motion.
- Underwater atmospheric effects.

Characters should not appear to float unnaturally unless the story requires it.

When a character enters water, their body position, animation, and water effects must respond accordingly.

Also create other appropriate effects when needed:

- Dust.
- Smoke.
- Sparks.
- Glowing effects.
- Falling particles.
- Rain.
- Wind.
- Environmental movement.

Effects must be visually clean and not overwhelm the scene.

## 11. VIDEO FORMAT AND TECHNICAL REQUIREMENTS

The final output must be optimized for vertical short-form platforms.

Target specifications:

- Aspect ratio: 9:16.
- Resolution: 1080 × 1920.
- Frame rate: 30 FPS or higher.
- Format: MP4.
- Codec: H.264.
- Audio: AAC.
- Duration: approximately 60–120 seconds, depending on the story.

Maintain consistent character proportions, lighting, color grading, and environment quality throughout the video.

Keep important characters and subtitles inside the vertical safe area.

Do not compose scenes as if they were landscape videos that have simply been cropped vertically.

All shots must be intentionally framed for a vertical screen.

## 13. WORKFLOW – HOW I WANT YOU TO WORK

Follow this sequence carefully.

**PHASE 1: REFERENCE ANALYSIS**

Study the attached reference video.

Analyze:
- Character appearance.
- Environmental design.
- Lighting.
- Materials.
- Camera angles.
- Camera movement.
- Character animation.
- Facial expressions.
- Subtitle design.
- Scene transitions.
- Audio and pacing.

Focus on extracting the production style, not copying the original story.

**PHASE 2: PRODUCTION DESIGN**

Before implementation, establish:

- A consistent visual style guide.
- Character specifications.
- Environment specifications.
- Animation requirements.
- Camera presets.
- Subtitle specifications.
- Audio requirements.

**PHASE 3: BUILD THE PRODUCTION SYSTEM**

Create the reusable environment, characters, animation library, camera system, and scene controller.

**PHASE 4: CREATE AN ORIGINAL STORY**

Develop an original short-form story using the same visual production style.

Create a complete storyboard with individual shots.

For every shot, specify:
- What happens.
- Which characters appear.
- What the characters do.
- Their facial expressions.
- Their exact positions.
- The camera angle.
- Camera movement.
- Duration.
- Dialogue.
- Subtitle.
- Sound effects.

**PHASE 5: IMPLEMENTATION**

Build and test the scenes.

Ensure that character animations, camera movements, dialogue, subtitles, and audio are synchronized.

**PHASE 6: RENDER AND QUALITY CONTROL**

Review the rendered video.

Check:
- Character consistency.
- Animation smoothness.
- Camera framing.
- Environment consistency.
- Subtitle synchronization.
- Audio synchronization.
- Visual artifacts.
- Frame rate.
- Vertical composition.

Fix problems before considering the video complete.

## 14. QUALITY STANDARD – VERY IMPORTANT

I want the final result to look like a professionally produced Roblox animated TikTok video.

Do not give me a basic Roblox gameplay recording.

Do not use static characters.

Do not use repetitive default animations for every scene.

Do not use random camera angles.

Do not create empty environments.

Do not use generic low-effort assets.

Do not make characters teleport between positions.

Do not make characters move without understanding what is happening in the scene.

Do not sacrifice animation quality for speed.

Prioritize visual storytelling, character acting, cinematic framing, and smooth editing.

The most important requirement is that the result should have the same overall visual identity and viewing experience as the reference video, while the story, dialogue, characters, and events can be entirely original.

**Start by analyzing the reference and the available development environment. Then propose the actual implementation plan. Do not skip directly to a simplified final result. Build a reusable production pipeline that can create multiple videos in this style.**
